Five Trays

Five directions for the tray that drops out of the notch. Each is a working mock: hover a provider to open its detail, move off the tray to close it, and press Replay to run the entrance again.

All five show the same sample data so the comparison is fair.

Codex 5% Claude 63% · live Kimi 87%
01

Instrument

A watch dial, not a chart. Hairline rings on a tick track, monospaced figures, the provider named in small capitals underneath.

Why it feels premium
  • Sixty ticks around each ring give the arc a scale to be read against, like a bezel.
  • Numerals are set in a real monospaced face, so 5, 63 and 87 line up to the pixel.
  • Nothing glows at rest. Colour is the one thing moving.
Motion
Arcs sweep in over 900 ms while the figures count up beneath them. The detail keeps the ring and unfolds two scaled hairlines with quarter ticks.
Risk
Quietest of the five. If you want the tray to be noticed, this is not the one.

02

Capsule

Three glass pills in the tray, the way Dynamic Island holds live activities. The hovered pill swallows the row.

Why it feels premium
  • Frosted pills with an inner top highlight read as material, not flat fills.
  • The provider mark sits in a tinted orb, so each pill carries its colour without a ring.
  • Hover is a morph, not a swap: the pill grows and the others fold to orbs.
Motion
Pills pop in with a stagger and a single light sweep crosses each. Focus is a spring on the pill's width, so the whole row rearranges in one motion.
Risk
Closest to Apple's own vocabulary, which reads as familiar or derivative depending on the customer.
Hover a pill for its detail
03

Concentric

One gauge, three nested arcs, a legend beside it. The most compact reading of all three providers at once.

Why it feels premium
  • A single object on the left and a typeset list on the right: a composed layout, not three copies of a tile.
  • Arcs open at the bottom like a speedometer, so the empty part of the ring says something.
  • Focus dims the other arcs instead of hiding them, so context stays on screen.
Motion
Arcs sweep outer to inner while legend rows slide in from the right. Hover swells the row and lifts its arc; the gauge centre takes the provider mark.
Risk
Inner arcs are short, so a low inner value reads smaller than the same value on the outer ring.
Hover a legend row
04

Ledger

No rings. The number is the design: three large figures set in columns, a hairline of progress under each.

Why it feels premium
  • Big tabular figures with tight tracking are the most legible reading from across a desk.
  • Hairline dividers and a single dot of colour per column keep it near monochrome.
  • It looks like a display on a piece of Braun equipment, not a widget.
Motion
Figures count up and hairlines draw. Hover widens the column, the neighbours recede, and the week line appears under the session line.
Risk
Drops the provider marks; the columns are told apart by name and colour only.
Hover a column
05

Segmented

Rings built from discrete cells that light up one by one, like a level meter on studio gear. Progress you can count.

Why it feels premium
  • Twenty-eight cells per ring make the fill feel measured rather than painted.
  • The last lit cell is white, a hot edge that marks exactly where you stand.
  • Detail bars use the same cells, so ring and bar are one system.
Motion
Cells light in sequence at 24 per second, a stepped fill instead of a smooth sweep. Hover keeps the ring and unfolds two cell bars beside it.
Risk
The most technical look. Reads as pro hardware, which may be too much for a casual user.
Hover a ring for its detail
